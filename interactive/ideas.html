The ideas that scaled, and the ideas that faded

Ten years of the Forbes Global 2000, sorted by what actually happened. Which business ideas took a bigger share of giant-company revenue and which lost ground, and which individual firms grew into the map and which shrank out of it.

What this measures. Growth is compound annual growth in the company's own reported revenue (or profit) between two years, not market value and not share price. A company's fiscal year rarely matches the calendar year, so the endpoints are approximate. And every company here is a survivor: it is on the 2026 list, so the firms that failed are missing from both ends. The "same firms only" view below is the honest like-for-like one — it compares the companies that reported in both years.

      How this was built. Same data as the year-by-year page: the Forbes Global 2000 (2026) list with each firm's own reported revenue and profit for fiscal years 2016 to 2025 (data/global2000_2026_history.csv). Compound growth is computed between the base and end year from those reported figures. Sector labels follow the 13-sector taxonomy used across the site, and the one-line "idea" for each sector comes from article/data/idea_sectors.csv.
