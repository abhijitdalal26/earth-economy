Twenty-five years, one picture

Every company a line. Time runs left to right, rank runs up the side — up is better. Lines rise into the top of the map, sink out of the bottom edge, and new lines appear when a company first has reported figures. And because each company is, in a sense, the idea it works in, the lines below the picture show which ideas have been in trend and which have faded.

How to read it. The vertical axis is rank within the firms that reported that measure that year (1 at the top, on a log scale). A line stops at the bottom edge when the company drops below the visible band, and climbs back in from the edge when it returns. Hover any line to see what the company actually does, when it grew and when it fell.

The history runs back to 2001 by stitching the Forbes Global 2000 editions (2011–2026): each edition carries ten years of financials, and firms are joined by a stable company id, so Facebook and Meta Platforms are the same line. Coverage thins in the early years (978 firms in 2001 → 1,999 in 2025). Every company here is a survivor on the 2026 list, so the firms that failed are missing entirely, and a year read from an older edition can differ from the same year in a newer one where Forbes restated it.

The ideas behind the lines

Each line is a business idea — a domain many companies share. The chart shows that idea's share of the tracked revenue, year by year: lines that climb are the ideas in trend, lines that sink are the ones that faded.

How this was built. The 2,000 companies of the Forbes Global 2000 (2026) list, with revenue, profit and headcount for fiscal years 2001 to 2025 stitched from the Forbes editions 2011–2026 (data/global2000_2026_history_panel.csv). Ranks are recomputed every year against every firm reporting that measure. Growth colouring is compound annual growth between a firm's first and last reported year. "What they do" comes from article/data/business_ideas.csv; the 13-sector taxonomy and the one-line idea for each sector are used across the site.
